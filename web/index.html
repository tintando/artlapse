    How it works

    Artlapse follows your canvas through the stream recording while you zoom, pan, rotate and flip it, and rebuilds the painting from what it sees. Then it makes a short video of the painting coming together. To do that it needs to know where to look, what to ignore and what to rebuild: three kinds of box you draw on the video.

    Workspace where you paint

    The part of the screen where your art program shows the canvas. Artlapse looks for the canvas only inside it. Leave out the program's menus, toolbars and panels. Without a Workspace, the whole screen is used.

    Exclude avatar, chat, etc.

    Things on top of the canvas that stay there: your avatar, the chat, a webcam. Whatever is under an exclusion is ignored, so it never gets painted into the timelapse. Draw as many as you need. Brief things, like alerts or the cursor, need no box: they don't stay long enough to be painted in.

    Target the canvas to rebuild

    The part of the canvas to rebuild, usually all of it: the timelapse shows exactly this box, the way it looks on the frame you draw it on. Pick a frame where the canvas is upright and not flipped, all of it is on screen inside the Workspace, and little of it is under an exclusion. Near the end of the stream often works: the painting is all there, so its edges are easy to see. The frame is marked on the timeline.

    Start, End and cuts

    No need to edit the recording first: when the stream shows something else, like a break screen or another program, Artlapse sees the canvas is gone and leaves that part out automatically. Start, End and cuts only make it faster, by leaving out what you know isn't painting, like the chatting before you start. Drag the white handles on the timeline, or use the = now buttons under it. To cut out a part, press ✂ Cut from here where it begins and End the cut here where it ends; drag a cut's ends to change it. The Target's frame can't be in a cut, or outside Start–End.

    Making the timelapse

    Once nothing is marked in red, press Make timelapse. It follows the canvas through the stream, rebuilds the painting, then makes the video, all on this device's graphics card (in a browser that can't use it, on the processor instead, much more slowly); a long stream takes a while, so keep this tab open. Made again after a change, it redoes only what the change affects: a new length only remakes the video. Save the video before you close the tab: it isn't kept.

    Mouse and keys

    Drag on the video to draw a box with the chosen tool. Click a box to select it, then drag it to move it, or drag its edges and corners. Del removes it, Esc deselects.

    This may be very slow here

    Artlapse does all its work in this browser, on this device: on its graphics card where the browser can use it, otherwise on its processor. On a phone or tablet either can be very slow, and the browser may stop the work when you switch to another app or lock the screen. A computer with Chrome or Edge does it much faster.
